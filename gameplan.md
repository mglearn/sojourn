## Suffrage Sojourn: five-level game plan

Concept: A no-combat historical adventure in which students travel through five time periods, gather primary-source artifacts, solve civic challenges, and unlock the path to the next milestone. Progress represents organizing and persistence, not defeating people.

| Level                   | Setting and historical focus                                                         | Player challenge                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Milestone earned    |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------- |
| 1. Declaration Hall     | Seneca Falls and the 1848 Declaration of Sentiments                                  | Match grievances with claims, assemble a declaration, and select evidence supporting voting rights                                                                                                                                   | Voice Badge         |
| 2. | 3. State Strategy Trail | State campaigns, western voting victories, and the 1918 Texas primary suffrage law   | Navigate a U.S. map, gather petition signatures, choose campaign strategies, and register fictional Texas voters before the deadline                                                                                                 | State Star          |
| 4. Ratification Run     | Congressional passage in 1919 and the state ratification campaign                    | Earn 36 ratification tokens by solving map, chronology, and legislative-process puzzles; complete the Tennessee challenge to open the certification chamber                                                                          | 19th Amendment Seal |
| 5. Equality Gate        | Voting access after 1920                                                             | Sort continuing barriers and later responses, including citizenship restrictions, Jim Crow, the white primary, and the Voting Rights Act; construct an evidence-backed explanation of why 1920 was a milestone rather than an ending | Equality Torch      |

### Recommended game loop

1. Explore a maze-like historical setting
2. Locate three evidence cards
3. Complete a short source-analysis puzzle
4. Use the evidence to solve the milestone challenge
5. Add the earned artifact to a visible journey map

Wrong answers should produce a hint or a short historical explanation. They should not remove lives or force students to restart the level.

### Grade-band modes

* K–2: Narrated directions, picture matching, simple sequencing, and classroom voting
* Grades 3–5: Short passages, timelines, maps, vocabulary help, and multiple-choice challenges
* Grades 6–8: Primary-source excerpts, cause-and-effect puzzles, and Texas history connections
* Grades 9–12: Sourcing, corroboration, competing perspectives, and evidence-supported claims

### Historical and instructional guardrails

* Include Black, Native American, Asian American, Latina, and white suffragists
* Explain disagreements and racism within the movement without asking students to reenact discrimination
* Treat laws, misleading claims, deadlines, and incomplete evidence as obstacles, not people
* Clearly distinguish constitutional ratification from actual access to the ballot
* Provide optional audio, captions, keyboard controls, reduced motion, and adjustable reading levels
* End with a source list and a teacher mode showing TEKS alignment and discussion questions
